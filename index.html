Turning multi-sensor Earth observations into interpretable science.

I’m a Research Scientist at Helmholtz-Zentrum Dresden-Rossendorf (HZDR), Germany, where I work on foundation models for Earth observation. I earned my Ph.D. and M.Eng. in Electrical Engineering from The University of Tokyo, specializing in explainable AI for remote sensing.

I’m drawn to problems where different sensors reveal different parts of the same story. Much of my work focuses on explainable fusion of SAR and multispectral optical imagery, with applications in disaster monitoring and the cryosphere. Currently, I contribute to the 3D-ABC foundation model, a Helmholtz initiative toward global above- and below-ground carbon stock mapping.

I care about how we communicate science. Through IEEE GRSS, I serve as a Webinar Strategist and Social Media Lead, where I turn complex research into clear, visual stories for the community. I also serve as a Column Editor for IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Magazine and review for journals including IEEE TGRS and JSTARS.

Teaching is my “parallel track.” Over the years, I have worn a few teaching hats. During my university years, I served as a student facilitator at the English Writing Center and worked as a teaching assistant for four years, supporting academic writing and presentations. I also taught physics and biology at a high school in Japan. These days, I occasionally join “Skype a Scientist” to speak with students. At Helmholtz, I occasionally teach machine learning courses to researchers as they navigate machine-learning concepts, practical workflows, and the “why”strong> behind modern deep-learning models. I also enjoy sharing this work beyond the institute through invited talks and community-facing lectures, where I translate technical ideas into accessible stories for diverse audiences. These conversations, whether with specialists or students, help me revisit the questions I had at the start and refine how I explain what we do. Teaching and speaking keep me grounded in fundamentals and purpose, so Earth observation feels less like a black box and more like a window.
